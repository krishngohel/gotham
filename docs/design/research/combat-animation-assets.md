## Decisions and why (one page for implementers)

**Combat feel (workstream I)**

1. **Fix finding 1 first, before anything else.** It is a missing `continue` in `reach.js`. It
   gives three of the most-used clips two contact times and no root motion, so any tuning done
   while the bug is still there is wasted.
2. **One source of truth for timing: event tracks on clips.** Every combat clip gets baked events
   (`warpStart`, `contact`, `cancel`, `recover`, `end`, plus `footL`/`footR`) in `mocapData.js`,
   generated by `retarget-mocap.mjs`. Hits fire when the playing action's time crosses `contact`,
   never from a separate timer. This fixes findings 1, 6, 8 and 12 and makes Nightwing and the
   co-op heroes correct without extra work.
3. **Replace `face()` snaps and the separate lunge curve with motion warping.** The clip's own root
   motion, measured in clip time, is skewed so that the limb arrives at the target on `contact`.
   Rotation is warped over the first 60–70 % of the window, translation over the whole window, and
   both are frozen from `contact` on. Warp distance is capped at about 2.5 m. Beyond that the
   strike is chosen from longer-reach clips, or a travel clip (dash or leap) plays first. A
   `Sprint_Loop` must never sit under a strike. Fixes findings 2, 5, 8 and 11.
4. **Use dead blending (a simple form of inertialization) for combat transitions instead of
   `crossFadeFrom`.** It runs as a post-pass after `mixer.update` on the 65 bone quaternions plus
   the pelvis position. It works on three.js r186 without changing the engine, and it only needs
   one action running at a time. Strike→strike transitions use 0.08–0.12 s, everything else
   crossfades as today. Fixes findings 3 and 4 and the dodge pop in 10. Apply it to heroes and the
   nearest 4 goons, and only on `medium`+ tiers.
5. **Bake trims; do not skip at runtime.** Large `MOCAP_START` values (finding 4) become baked
   start trims (`%start` in the retarget script). The baked clip then begins in a pose that suits
   being blended into.
6. **Hit-stop becomes per-actor, not global.** `time.hitStop` freezes the whole world
   (`src/core/time.js`). In split screen that stalls the other player on every hit; in online
   co-op it cannot be replicated. Freeze the attacker's and victim's animation and movement clocks
   only. Keep global slow motion for single-player last-hit moments and replace it in co-op with
   per-view camera effects. **The design doc does not address this; it should.**
7. **Reactions run to their `recover` event and are chosen by direction.** Build a 4-direction ×
   3-severity reaction table (light, heavy, knockdown) with `~mirror` variants. Turn the victim
   toward the attacker over 0.1 s for front reactions, or use a back-reaction clip. Light hits on
   brutes, shields and the hero mid-move play as additive flinches (`AnimationUtils.makeClipAdditive`).
   Airborne goons get an air-hit pose. Fixes findings 7 and 9.
8. **Keep the existing attack director and add tokens, rings and per-player budgets.** Tokens cost
   by attack weight. Each player gets their own budget and, separately, there is a ring of
   positions with slots. Enemies without a token must still move: strafe, taunt, pick up weapons.

**Enemies (I, J)**

9. **Add roles that each force a different answer:** shield, stun baton, armour, knife (existing),
   medic, sniper, riot gunner, brute (existing), martial artist, dog, drone and lieutenant. Add no
   more than one new type per mission. Scale difficulty through mixes, tell length, token budgets
   and counter windows, **not by giving enemies more health.** The design doc's "enemy counts and
   health scale with player count" (section 4.1) should become "counts and tokens scale; health
   scales by at most 1.25×".

## 1. What makes freeflow melee feel smooth, mapped to our findings

### 1.1 Motion warping instead of snap + lunge

**What the reference games do.** Freeflow combat picks a strike from a set authored for different
distances and directions. The engine then bends the clip's root motion so the blow lands on the
chosen enemy. The motion still reads as the animator's motion, only stretched. Unreal's Motion
Warping formalises this. A *warping window* is a notify state inside a montage. A named *warp
target* (position + rotation) is updated from gameplay. The *Skew Warp* modifier adjusts root
motion so that at the end of the window the root matches the target, and `bWarpRotation` and a
rotation time multiplier control rotation separately from translation
([UE docs](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/motion-warping-in-unreal-engine),
[URootMotionModifier_Warp](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/API/Plugins/MotionWarping/URootMotionModifier_Warp)).
Camouflaj gave a GDC 2026 talk on carrying freeflow into *Batman: Arkham Shadow*, "Punching Across
the Room"
([session page](https://schedule.gdconf.com/session/punching-across-the-room-in-batman-arkham-shadow/914380),
abstract not fetched: 403). The title alone shows that long-distance strike travel is a core
problem in the genre. **[unverified: talk contents]**

**What we do today.** `approach()` (`combatSystem.js:232-309`) already computes a good plan: the
limb offset, clip root motion and a scale `k`, plus target drift. Three things break it:

- yaw is snapped (`face`, 375 and 394);
- the lunge runs on its own curve in *game* time, separate from the clip, which peaks at twice the
  average speed;
- long approaches play `Sprint_Loop` at 1.3× and then start the strike late.

**Retrofit.** Keep `approach()`'s maths and change how it is *applied*.

```
// per frame, while clipTime in [warpStart, contact]
rem      = rm(contact) - rm(clipTime)                 // remaining authored root motion (clip space → world by yaw)
need     = targetContactPos - hero.pos                // where the root must be on the contact frame
frac     = (clipTime1 - clipTime0) / (contact - clipTime0)   // share of the window this frame
delta    = rmDelta(clipTime0, clipTime1) + (need - rem) * frac   // skew warp
yawStep  = angleDiff(yaw, targetYaw) * min(1, (clipTime1 - clipTime0) / (rotateUntil - clipTime0))
```

- Rotation finishes at `rotateUntil` (about 60–70 % of the window). The limb then travels straight
  into the target, which reads better than turning on impact.
- Clamp `|need - rem|` to `maxWarp` (2.5 m for strikes, 4 m for flying moves). If the target is
  further away, the move selector should pick a travel move (dash, `Knee_Flying`, `Kick_Flying`,
  `Smash_Leap`) whose authored root motion covers the distance. This is the freeflow trick: a
  longer move is chosen, not a faster lunge.
- Track target drift until `contact`, as `place()` already does.
- Zero `hero.vel` on strike entry (finding 11).
- Enemies use the same warp for their attack lunge (finding 8). Damage is resolved at `contact`
  against the *current* distance, and the hero dodges out of the warp window. That gives the
  Arkham "dodge at the last moment" feel for free.

Maps to findings **2, 5, 8, 11** (and finding 1's root data).

### 1.2 Animation-event hit frames, cancel windows and recovery

Hit timing must come from the clip, because a clip's speed, start trim and blend all move the
contact frame. Today `impactAt` is a timer computed from `reach[clip].contact`, and different
systems read different contact values (finding 1, `chainTimeline.js:98`). Nightwing uses a fixed
0.22 s (finding 12) and air kicks rescale speed (finding 6).

Use one event table per clip, generated at bake time:

| event | meaning | used by |
|---|---|---|
| `warpStart` | motion warping window opens | warp |
| `contact` | damage, hit-stop, reaction | combat resolution, enemies, allies, chains |
| `cancel` | from here a buffered strike/counter/dodge may transition | input buffer |
| `recover` | from here any action, including locomotion | state machine |
| `end` | natural end; go to combat idle | state machine |
| `footL`/`footR` | foot plant | footstep audio, dust, foot lock |

**Cancel conventions.** Counter and dodge are allowed until `contact - 0.02` (this is today's
`interruptible`; keep it). Strike→strike is allowed from `cancel`, which should be about
`contact + hitStop + 0.06–0.10 s`, not 40 ms after contact as today (finding 3). Freeflow's speed
comes from cancelling *into the next strike's wind-up*, so the cancel itself is right. What is
missing is a blend that hides it (see 1.3). Keep an input buffer of about 0.2–0.3 s so a press
during the follow-through is honoured at `cancel`.

Maps to findings **1, 3, 6, 9, 12**.

### 1.3 Inertialization vs cross-fade, on three.js

**Background.** Inertialization (Bollo, *Gears of War*, GDC 2018) switches to the destination
animation immediately and fades out the *offset* between the old and new pose over a short time,
so only one animation is evaluated
([GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1025165/Inertialization-High-Performance-Animation-Transitions),
[Game Developer summary](https://gamedeveloper.com/art/video-high-performance-animation-transitions-in-i-gears-of-war-i-)).
Holden's spring version decays the offset with a critically damped spring and a half-life
([Spring-It-On, 2021-04-03](https://theorangeduck.com/page/spring-roll-call)). His *dead blending*
variant extrapolates the *source* pose forward using its velocity, with decay, and cross-fades from
that extrapolation into the destination. It only needs the current pose and velocity at
transition time
([Dead Blending, 2023-02-25](https://theorangeduck.com/page/dead-blending)).

**Why it helps here.** `crossFadeFrom` blends two live clips. When the source is a strike that is
mid-swing, the fade drags the arm back toward wherever the old clip is *going*. When the
destination starts mid-wind-up (`startAt`), the blend passes through a mush of two poses.
Inertialization keeps the arm's *momentum* and lands in the new clip's pose, which is what a cancel
should look like.

**Practical on AnimationMixer?** Yes, as a post-pass. three.js has no built-in support
(confirmed: no inertialization in r186 `src/animation`). The mixer writes `bone.quaternion` and
`pelvis.position` during `mixer.update`. We read them back and overwrite them before rendering.

```js
// src/actors/deadBlend.js — sketch, allocation-free per frame (rule 5)
import * as THREE from 'three';
export function createDeadBlend(bones, pelvis, { decayHalfLife = 0.05 } = {}) {
  const n = bones.length;
  const last = new Float32Array(n * 4), prev = new Float32Array(n * 4);  // last two output poses
  const ext = new Float32Array(n * 4), w = new Float32Array(n * 3);      // extrapolated pose, angular vel
  const lastP = new THREE.Vector3(), prevP = new THREE.Vector3(), extP = new THREE.Vector3(), vP = new THREE.Vector3();
  const qa = new THREE.Quaternion(), qb = new THREE.Quaternion(), qd = new THREE.Quaternion();
  let t = 0, dur = 0, lastDt = 1 / 60;
  return {
    // Call right before the state machine switches actions (no crossFade: new action weight 1).
    start(blend = 0.1) {
      for (let i = 0; i < n; i++) {
        qa.fromArray(last, i * 4); qb.fromArray(prev, i * 4);
        qd.copy(qa).multiply(qb.invert());                    // rotation over the last frame
        if (qd.w < 0) { qd.x = -qd.x; qd.y = -qd.y; qd.z = -qd.z; qd.w = -qd.w; }
        const s = Math.sqrt(Math.max(0, 1 - qd.w * qd.w)), ang = 2 * Math.acos(Math.min(1, qd.w));
        const k = s > 1e-6 ? ang / s / lastDt : 0;
        w[i * 3] = qd.x * k; w[i * 3 + 1] = qd.y * k; w[i * 3 + 2] = qd.z * k;
        ext.set(last.subarray(i * 4, i * 4 + 4), i * 4);
      }
      extP.copy(lastP); vP.copy(lastP).sub(prevP).divideScalar(lastDt);
      t = 0; dur = blend;
    },
    // Call after mixer.update(dt), before render. dt = this actor's local dt (0 during its hit-stop).
    apply(dt) {
      if (dt <= 0) return;
      if (t < dur) {
        t += dt;
        const decay = Math.pow(0.5, dt / decayHalfLife);
        const a = t / dur, alpha = a * a * (3 - 2 * a);
        for (let i = 0; i < n; i++) {
          const wx = w[i * 3] *= decay, wy = w[i * 3 + 1] *= decay, wz = w[i * 3 + 2] *= decay;
          const ang = Math.hypot(wx, wy, wz) * dt;
          qa.fromArray(ext, i * 4);
          if (ang > 1e-7) { const s = Math.sin(ang / 2) / (ang / dt); qd.set(wx * s, wy * s, wz * s, Math.cos(ang / 2)); qa.premultiply(qd); }
          qa.toArray(ext, i * 4);
          const q = bones[i].quaternion;
          if (qa.dot(q) < 0) { qa.x = -qa.x; qa.y = -qa.y; qa.z = -qa.z; qa.w = -qa.w; }  // same hemisphere
          q.copy(qa.slerp(q, alpha));
        }
        vP.multiplyScalar(decay); extP.addScaledVector(vP, dt);
        pelvis.position.lerpVectors(extP, pelvis.position, alpha);
      }
      prev.set(last); prevP.copy(lastP);
      for (let i = 0; i < n; i++) bones[i].quaternion.toArray(last, i * 4);
      lastP.copy(pelvis.position); lastDt = dt;
    },
  };
}
```

Notes:

- `animator.play` gains a `blend: 'inertial'` option. It stops the old action
  (`setEffectiveWeight(0)` / `stop()`), starts the new one at weight 1, and calls `deadBlend.start()`.
- The twin-action trick in `animator.js` is no longer needed for inertial transitions.
- Typical blend times are 0.08–0.12 s for strike→strike and strike→react, and 0.15–0.2 s for
  react→idle.
- Clamp the extrapolation: a decay half-life of about 0.05 s. Holden warns that long extrapolations
  produce "crazy poses".
- The cost is 65 slerps per blending actor per frame, which is trivial. Gate it with
  `quality.anim.inertial`. On `potato`/`low`, fall back to crossfade.
- Root motion is not blended. The warp owns the root, and `pelvis.position` holds only the
  in-place hips (vertical motion and residual sway), as the retarget script already arranges.

Maps to findings **3, 4, 7 (reaction restarts), 10 (dodge pop)**.

### 1.4 Hit-stop conventions

- **Durations.** Sakurai's column describes hit-stop as freezing *both* attacker and defender for
  the same time, longer for stronger hits but capped. Fighting games sometimes give the defender
  a few extra frames on counter-hits
  ([Sakurai column, fan translation](https://sourcegaming.info/2015/11/11/thoughts-on-hitstop-sakurais-famitsu-column-vol-490-1/),
  [Dustloop GGST](https://dustloop.com/w/GGST/Attack_Attributes)).
- **Pose.** *God of War* (2018) pops the target into its hit pose and holds both Kratos and the
  target on that first frame
  ([PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/?p=370399)).
- **Our values are already in that range.** `STOP = { punch: 0.05, kick: 0.065, heavy: 0.11,
  finisher: 0.13, counter: 0.1 }` matches 3–8 frames at 60 Hz. Keep them.

**Per-actor vs global, and co-op.** `time.hitStop` subtracts from the *world* dt
(`src/core/time.js:27-33`), so traffic, other goons, particles and the *other player* all freeze.
At freeflow tempo (3–4 hits/s), 20 % or more of wall time is frozen. In split screen that is felt
as input lag by player 2. Online, the host cannot freeze a guest's local hero.

Decision:

- Add an `actor.clock` (`localScale`, `stopT`). `hitStop(actor, sec)` freezes the attacker and
  victim mixers (`mixer.update(0)`) and their warp/root motion.
- The world, the other player and the camera keep running. The camera shake and FOV kick play
  during the freeze, so the freeze is still felt.
- `slowMo` stays global only when `players.length === 1`. In co-op, critical or last-hit moments
  become per-view effects: zoom, speed lines, a desaturation pulse.
- The net layer sends hit-stop as part of the `hit` event, so both peers freeze the same two actors.

### 1.5 Directional hit reactions and additive flinches

Hit reactions sell the hit more than the strike does. *God of War*'s axe only "became playful"
once varied hit reactions went in
([GamerRevolution](https://www.gamerevolution.com/originals/525661-god-of-war-axe-origins-anniversary)).
Sifu's team re-authored mocap impacts by hand because actors never connect
([Digital Trends interview](https://digitaltrends.com/gaming/sloclap-sifu-animation-interview)).

**Reaction table.**

- **Light hits** use front, back, left and right clips, picked from
  `dot(hitDir, victimForward)` and `cross`. Existing assets: `Hit_Head`, `Hit_Head_Snap`,
  `Hit_Chest`, `Hit_Gut_Fold`, `Hit_Spin`, mirrored with `~mirror`.
- **Heavy hits** use stagger-back clips (`Hit_Knockback`).
- **Knockdowns** use fall clips by direction (`Fall_Flat`, `Sweep_Fall`), plus a get-up
  (`Goon_GetUp`, `LayToIdle`).
- **Launches** use an air-hit loop. This fixes the standing flinch played in the air (finding 7).
- **Facing.** Before playing a *front* reaction, turn the victim toward the attacker over 0.08 s.
  A hit from behind plays a *back* clip and does not turn him.

**Durations.** Bake light reactions to 0.35–0.5 s (trim and ease back to fight idle) and heavy
ones to 0.7–0.9 s. The FSM leaves `hit` on `recover`, not on a 0.5 s timer. Today the goon leaves
`hit` at 0.5 s while the clip runs for 1.2–1.6 s, which cuts the clip off mid-motion.

**Additive flinches.** Use them for hits that do not interrupt: brute, armour, shield block, and
the hero being chipped during an uninterruptible move. Make them with
`AnimationUtils.makeClipAdditive(clip, 0)` (present in r186 at
`node_modules/three/src/animation/AnimationUtils.js:263`) on the first 0.3 s of a reaction clip.
Play at weight 0.5–0.8 on top of whatever is playing. This is three.js's documented additive path
([example](https://threejs.org/examples/#webgl_animation_skinning_additive_blending)).

Maps to findings **7, 9**.

### 1.6 Camera

- **Frame the fight, not the hero.** In combat, put the pivot between the hero and the centroid of
  engaged enemies, weighted toward the current target. Widen distance and FOV with spread.
  Critically damp all camera motion. Never let a target switch rotate the camera by itself.
- **Shake.** Make it directional along the hit vector and short (`hitKick` already exists). Scale
  it by severity. It runs during the per-actor freeze.
- **Off-screen attackers** must be announced by the counter indicator and audio (see 3.5). Do not
  swing the camera to show them.
- **Co-op.** Each view frames its own player. On a shared screen (if ever), frame both players
  plus the enemy centroid.

### 1.7 Foot IK

This has low value for flat streets once warping removes the slide. The cases where it is worth
doing:

- foot *lock* during the warp window;
- stairs and rooftop edges in combat.

three.js ships `CCDIKSolver` (present in `node_modules/three/examples/jsm/animation/`). For legs, a
two-bone analytic IK is cheaper and more stable. Make it `high`/`ultra` only, and later.

## 2. A minimal animation graph for three.js (retrofit size)

**Goal.** Callers ask for *intents* (`strike(clipId, target)`, `react(dir, severity)`,
`locomote(speed)`), and the graph owns clip choice, events, blending and the root. Keep it to one
file of about 300 lines plus data.

**State data** (`src/actors/animStates.js`, pure, testable):

```js
export const STATES = {
  fightIdle: { clip: 'Goon_Fight_Idle', loop: true },
  loco:      { blend1D: [['Idle_Loop', 0], ['Walk_Loop', 1.6], ['Jog_Fwd_Loop', 4], ['Sprint_Loop', 8]] },
  strike:    { once: true, warp: { maxDist: 2.5, rotateUntil: 0.65 }, rootMotion: true,
               exits: { strike: 'cancel', counter: 'start', dodge: 'cancel', react: 'start', '*': 'recover' } },
  react:     { once: true, exits: { counter: 'recover', '*': 'recover' } },
  airReact:  { clip: 'Hit_Air_Loop', loop: true, exits: { land: 'start' } },
  down:      { once: true, exits: { getUp: 'end' } },
};
// Per-clip events come from MOCAP_DATA (baked): { warpStart, contact, cancel, recover, end, footL:[], footR:[] }
```

**Runtime.**

- `graph.request(state, params)` checks the current state's `exits[state]` against the event
  already passed and either transitions or buffers the request (0.25 s buffer).
- On transition it calls `animator.play(clip, { blend: inertial|fade })`.
- `graph.update(dt)`:
  1. advances the actor clock (hit-stop aware);
  2. reads `action.time` before and after `mixer.update`;
  3. fires every event in `(t0, t1]`, handling loops and `timeScale`;
  4. applies root motion and warp to the actor position through the collision resolver (as
     `place()` does today);
  5. runs `deadBlend.apply`.
- Events go out on the existing `events` bus (`animEvent`, `{ actor, name, clip }`). Combat listens
  for `contact`. Audio and VFX listen for `foot*`.

**Layers and bone masks in AnimationMixer.** This is the non-obvious part. A three.js mixer
normalises weights *per property*: two full-body actions at weight 1 average 50/50, and there are
no per-bone weights on an action. To make an upper-body overlay (a gadget throw or block while
running, a taunt while strafing), **split clips into masked sub-clips**:

```js
const UPPER = new Set(['spine_01','spine_02','spine_03','neck_01','head', /* clavicles, arms, hands */]);
const mask = (clip, keep, suffix) => new THREE.AnimationClip(clip.name + suffix, clip.duration,
  clip.tracks.filter((t) => keep(t.name.slice(0, t.name.lastIndexOf('.')))));
// base locomotion plays as two actions: Walk@lower + Walk@upper.
// overlay: fade Walk@upper → Throw@upper (weight 1) while Walk@lower keeps running.
```

- The base layer is full body (lower and upper actions at weight 1).
- The upper overlay crossfades *only against the base's upper action*.
- The additive layer is independent (`AdditiveAnimationBlendMode`) and so needs no masking.
- Create masked clips lazily and cache them per clip name. `animator.prime` should prime both halves.
- three.js's
  [skinning blending example](https://threejs.org/examples/#webgl_animation_skinning_blending) shows
  the weight model. Masking by filtering tracks is a common community pattern **[no official
  example verified]**.

**Root motion extraction.** It already exists. `retarget-mocap.mjs` removes horizontal hips travel
from the clip and writes it per frame to `mocapData.js`, and `rootMotionAt()` samples it. Extend
the same output with the event fields. For UAL clips (no mocap data), add a bake step that extracts
pelvis XZ from the GLB clip the same way, so every combat clip has root and events. Without that
step, the warp has nothing to bend.

## 3. Enemy AI

### 3.1 Tokens, rings and slots

The existing `director.js` caps simultaneous wind-ups (2), gives a per-enemy cooldown (2.2–4 s) and
spaces starts (0.35 s). That is a token system with one token type. Industry references:

- id's DOOM (2016) used per-attack-type tokens so only a limited number of enemies may use an
  attack at once
  ([GDC 2018 "Embracing Push Forward Combat in DOOM"](https://gdcvault.com/play/1024940/Embracing-Push-Forward-Combat-in);
  token detail from secondary sources **[talk itself not viewed]**).
- Insomniac's *Spider-Man* combat manager handed out "attack jobs". A closer enemy could take one
  over from a distant enemy, and extra jobs were made when the player stayed close
  ([GDC 2019 AI postmortem](https://gdcvault.com/play/1025828/-Marvel-s-Spider-Man),
  [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/designing-ai-to-do-anything-a-spider-can-in-i-marvel-s-spider-man-i-);
  detail from a third-party write-up **[unverified]**).
- The "Kung-Fu Circle" from *Kingdoms of Amalur* organises approach positions around the player
  ([Packt recipe summary](https://subscription.packtpub.com/book/game-development/9781788626170/5/ch05lvl1sec60/building-a-fighting-circle);
  original talk **[not found]**).
- In short: **tokens fix timing; slots fix space**.

**Proposed upgrade (pure module, Vitest):**

- **Token budget per player.** `budget = base(difficulty) × weight`. Costs: light melee 1, heavy or
  unblockable 2, grab 2, ranged shot 1 (a separate ranged pool). An enemy requests its attack's
  cost and gets all or nothing.
- **Rings per player:**
  - inner (1.8–2.6 m): 4–6 angular slots, for token holders and the next in line;
  - outer (4–7 m): strafers who taunt, pick up weapons or flank;
  - ranged (10–20 m): needs line of sight.
  Assign slots with a greedy angular cost (distance + angle change + crowding).
- **Steal rule** (from Spider-Man): an enemy in the inner ring may take a token from an enemy who
  is still approaching from more than 4 m away.
- **Flanking.** Prefer free slots behind the player's facing for the *next* token holder. An attack
  from behind is legal only if the counter indicator shows at least 0.35 s before contact (tells
  in 3.5).
- **Pressure valve.** If the player has not been attacked for 2.5 s, add +1 temporary token.
  Spider-Man's "player stays close" rule is similar.

### 3.3 Cheap behaviours that read as "smart"

- **Intent barks before acting** ("Get behind him!", "Medic!", "Take the shot!"). This is the
  single cheapest trick, and the bark API is already planned in workstream E.
- Pick up dropped weapons (knives, pipes, guns) when in the outer ring.
- React to KOs: brief hesitation, look at the body, morale checks. The last goon or two may flee,
  surrender, or call backup.
- Avoid friendly fire: gunners reposition when a goon blocks the line.
- Dodge a telegraphed batarang 20–30 % of the time (martial artist 60 %).
- Surround *visibly*: slow approach, spreading out, taunts.
- Retreat when hurt; regroup around the lieutenant.
- Remember the player's last tactic (e.g. many aerial attacks → shields face upward). One counter
  per fight is enough to feel adaptive.

### 3.4 Two players

- `pickTarget(enemy, players)` uses a score:
  `−distance + 3·(hitMeRecently) + 2·(isMyCurrentTarget, hysteresis 3–5 s) − 1.5·(tokensAlreadyOnThatPlayer) − LOS penalty`.
  Ties go to the player with fewer enemies engaged.
- Each player has their own token budget and rings. Total attack density scales about 1.6–1.8×
  for two players, not 2×. Freeflow relies on the counter indicator being readable, and a crowd
  that is twice as dense stops being readable.
- **Downed player.** Most enemies shift to the standing player (capped by that player's budget).
  One or two "guard" the downed player, which pressures the revive. That is a good co-op moment.
- **Scaling.** Enemy count ×1.5, special roles +1, tokens ×1.6–1.8, health ×1.0–1.25. Bullet
  sponges make freeflow tedious.
- **Net.** Token grants are host-owned events. Guests never grant tokens. Counter-indicator timing
  must be shown from the host's `windup` event, plus measured latency, on the guest.

### 3.5 Difficulty for a longer game, without cheapness

- **Fairness rules** (test them):
  - every attack telegraphs for at least T before contact (normal 0.45 s, hard 0.35 s);
  - no more than 2 unblockable attacks overlap;
  - off-screen attacks always show the indicator and play spatial audio;
  - no damage during a hero's uninterruptible finisher unless that attack was already
    telegraphed before it started.
- **Levers, in order of preference:**
  1. enemy mix and role combinations;
  2. tell length / counter window;
  3. token budget;
  4. fewer free heals (combo-earned heals only);
  5. indicator visibility on the hardest tier (Arkham-style "no counter icons" as an optional
     modifier);
  6. damage;
  7. health (last).
- **Curve across acts.** Introduce a role, test it alone, then combine it. Each act adds one rule.
  Boss fights reuse the token system with boss-specific budgets.

## 5. Making low-poly heroes read better (toon + ink)

- **Silhouette first.** At game distance the outline *is* the character.
  - Batman: taller, sharper cowl ears; a cape as a separate skinned sheet with 4–6 bone chains
    driven by verlet springs (cheap; quality-tiered); gauntlet fins; a chunky belt; broader
    shoulders and bigger hands than real proportions.
  - Nightwing: slimmer and longer-legged, escrima sticks on the back, hair spikes as rigid parts.
  - Alfred: tailcoat tails (rigid or two-bone), a narrow silhouette, bald crown, moustache
    primitive.
  - Robin: short cape and a high collar.
  - Superman: a long cape is the whole read. Hair curl primitive.
  - Civilians: hats, bags, coats and umbrellas as swap-in rigid parts. These are cheaper than new
    bodies and read further away.
- **Inner lines in the texture.** `paint-suits.mjs` already paints regions. Add 2–3 px ink lines at
  panel seams, muscle-group edges and boot and glove tops, plus one hard dark toon band per muscle
  group. This is comic inking and costs nothing at runtime.
- **Face and cowl.** Model the cowl as a separate shell over the head: sharp brow ridge, white eye
  lenses (unshaded emissive), exposed jaw with a strong chin plane. For unmasked heads (Alfred,
  Bruce, civilians), add 2–3 painted lines (brow, nose underside, mouth) rather than modelling.
- **Rim light.** A fresnel step in `toon.js` gives a cool rim on the night side. It separates dark
  suits from dark streets, which matters most for Batman. Add a narrow specular band on the cowl
  and boots as comic "sheen".
- **Outline tuning.** Make outlines thicker on silhouette parts (cape, ears) and thinner on face
  and hands, using per-material thickness or vertex-colour weight for the inverted hull. Tune
  depth and normal edge thresholds so cape folds ink but the face does not get noisy.
- **Emblems and colour blocking.** Use few, large, flat colour regions. Emblems are flat decal
  planes, which are crisper than painted texels. Character emblems are trademarks; see 7.2.

## 6. Sound

**Current state.** Everything is synthesised (`CREDITS.md`). Synthesis is great for UI, music
stingers and variety. It struggles with body impacts, cloth, gravel and engines, which is where
"crunchy" combat comes from.

**Decision.** Keep a hybrid. Each impact is a sampled transient (CC0 punch, slap or body hit) plus
the existing synth "thump" and a pitch and EQ variation per hit. Add whooshes keyed to `contact`
minus about 0.08 s from the event track, footsteps from `foot*` events by surface, body falls on
knockdowns, and vehicle loops with RPM pitch and ambience beds per district.

**Sources (CC0 first):**

- Kenney Impact Sounds (130 files, CC0) and other Kenney audio packs.
- Freesound with the CC0 filter; CC-BY only with credit.
- OpenGameArt CC0.
- Sonniss bundles: royalty-free, but **not** as separate files in a public repo (see the table).

**Web formats (Nov 2026).** Opus is supported in Chrome, Firefox and Edge. Safari plays Opus in
**WebM** from 17.5 (macOS and iOS), and iOS 18.4+ is listed as full support. Before 17.5, Safari
needs a CAF container
([caniuse data](https://github.com/Fyrd/caniuse/blob/main/features-json/opus.json)).
caniuse covers the `<audio>` element. Support in Web Audio `decodeAudioData` is likely but
**[unverified]**, so test it on Safari. Ship:

- SFX as `.webm` Opus, mono, 48–64 kbps;
- ambience/music as stereo, 96 kbps;
- an MP3 fallback only if pre-2024 Safari is a target, chosen with `canPlayType`.

At those rates, 150 one-shots of about 0.6 s come to roughly 0.7 MB. Lazy-load district ambience
to respect the 40 MB `dist/` budget.

## 7. Legal and licence detail

### 7.1 Mixamo in a public repo

Facts:

- The repo is public on GitHub, `private: true` in `package.json` only, with no LICENSE file.
- `public/assets/anims_mocap.glb` (56 clips) and `src/config/mocapData.js` hold Mixamo-derived
  motion retargeted to our skeleton.
- Raw FBX is git-ignored (`assets-src/`), as rule 7 requires.

Adobe's terms: use in games is clearly allowed. Redistributing "raw" files is not, and that
includes free distribution. The current official FAQ could not be read (403), so this rests on the
2022 forum copy and on how games commonly ship Mixamo motion.

My reading: retargeted, trimmed, resampled clips baked into the game's own asset is the intended
use. A public repo where anyone can download the GLB is a grey area, not a clear breach. Low risk;
it is Adobe's call.

To keep it defensible:

1. Credit Mixamo in `CREDITS.md`, with a line saying the clips are licensed for this game only and
   not for reuse.
2. Never put a licence on the repo that covers `public/assets/**` or `mocapData.js`. If code is
   ever open-sourced, licence `src/` only and exclude asset data explicitly.
3. Keep raw FBX out (done).
4. Do not publish the clip set as a separate pack.

### 7.2 DC characters in an unofficial fan game

Plainly: Batman, Nightwing, Robin, Superman, the Joker, Harley Quinn, Gotham and their emblems are
DC/Warner Bros. copyrights and trademarks. An unofficial game using them is unlicensed use. A
disclaimer and "non-commercial" do **not** make it legal. They signal good faith and reduce the
chance and cost of action
([Seeles](https://www.seeles.ai/resources/blogs/ai-fan-game-copyright-risks),
[Game Developer, attorney Mona Ibrahim](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/analysis-clone-games-fan-games----legal-issues)).

Warner Bros. has sent cease-and-desists to DC fan projects:

- the *Dark Knight: Legacy* crowdfunded fan film (2013)
  ([Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2013/09/26/dark-knight-legacy-cease-and-desist-warner-bros/));
- an Arrowverse fan game (2016, per a fan petition)
  ([change.org](https://www.change.org/p/mark-pedowitz-save-crisis-on-earth-one-arrowverse-fan-game)).

The realistic risk is a takedown (a C&D or a DMCA notice to GitHub or the host) that ends
distribution. A lawsuit against a free, non-commercial project is unlikely but possible.

What keeps a project on the safer side:

- **No money of any kind:** no ads, donations, Patreon or crowdfunding tied to it, and no paid
  builds.
- **Original assets only:** no ripped models, audio or music, and no copies of Arkham levels, UI or
  enemy designs. The design doc already says this.
- **No official logos or wordmarks.** Use our own bat-symbol and Superman-shield takes, and do not
  use "Arkham" or WB/DC branding.
- A clear "unofficial, not affiliated" notice on the title screen and site (already in `CREDITS.md`).
- Do not market it as a product, and comply quickly if contacted.
- **Keep names and branding as data**, so that a takedown could be answered by renaming to original
  characters rather than deleting the project. This is a cheap architectural hedge and fits the
  roster's "kit is data" contract.
- **Voices:** do not clone or imitate a specific actor's voice for the Joker or others. That adds
  right-of-publicity exposure. **[I have not checked how the Seed Audio lines were produced.]**

### 7.3 Other flags

- **Quaternius licence drift.** Assets already in the repo were obtained under CC0, and CC0 cannot
  be revoked. QAL section 7 also says terms are not retroactive. New downloads may come under the
  QAL, which bans redistributing assets "as a standalone asset". As with Mixamo, raw GLBs in a
  public repo are a grey area. Note the download date for each pack in `CREDITS.md`.
- **Meshy/Higgsfield kicks:** check the terms of the service that supplied the motion and whether
  it was trained on or sourced from third-party mocap **[unverified]**.
- **Poly Haven** appears in rule 7's "preferred" list but is photo-real. Rule 6 bans photo
  textures, so drop it from the preferred list or limit it to HDRI lighting reference.

## 8. Disagreements with the design docs

1. **Section 2, rule 7: "CC0 (Quaternius …)"** is out of date for new downloads; see 7.3.
2. **Section 2, rule 7: Poly Haven as "preferred"** conflicts with rule 6.
3. **Sections 4.1/4.4 say nothing about time control.** Global hit-stop and slow motion break split
   screen and cannot be replicated online. Add "time effects are per-actor or per-view" to the
   contract before workstream D starts.
4. **Section 4.1: "enemy counts and health scale with player count."** Scale counts and tokens;
   keep health scaling at 1.25× or less.
5. **Section 3, Characters: "not from foreign rigs."** Agreed for bodies. For *animation*, a
   one-off bone map per source (CMU, 100STYLE, KayKit) is cheap and widens the library a lot; allow
   it.
6. **combat-findings.md, finding 3** reads as "cancels are too early". Freeflow *needs* early
   cancels. The fault is the blend and the missing `cancel` event, not the cancel itself. Do not
   fix it by making strikes play out longer.
7. **combat-findings.md, structural causes.** Add "global hit-stop" to the list; it is structural
   for co-op.
